# Caltech ᗒ⟋ᗕ߉⟍ᗒᔕ ### The Philosophy of Mathematics of Dynamical Reality **Caltech ᗒ⟋ᗕ߉⟍ᗒᔕ** begins with the proposition: > **Mathematics is the language in which change becomes structure.** The philosophy does not regard mathematical objects as fundamentally static. Instead, it begins with: $$ x(t) $$ and asks: $$ \boxed{\text{How does mathematical structure evolve?}} $$ Its symbols represent a sequence of ideas: $$ \boxed{ \mathsf{ᗒ} \rightarrow \mathsf{\⟋} \rightarrow \mathsf{ᗕ} \rightarrow \mathsf{߉} \rightarrow \mathsf{\⟍} \rightarrow \mathsf{ᗒ} \rightarrow \mathsf{\ᔕ} } $$ --- # I. ᗒ — State The first symbol represents a mathematical state. A system at time \(t\) is represented by $$ x(t)\in X. $$ The space \(X\) contains all mathematically possible states of the system. Thus: $$ \boxed{ \text{Reality begins as a state space of possibilities.} } $$ --- # II. ⟋ — Evolution The symbol $$ \mathsf{\⟋} $$ represents transformation through time. A dynamical law may take the form $$ \frac{dx}{dt}=F(x,t). $$ Or, in discrete time: $$ x_{n+1}=F(x_n). $$ The mathematical object is therefore not merely \(x\), but the pair: $$ \boxed{(X,F)}. $$ A state without a law of evolution is incomplete from the Caltech perspective. --- # III. ᗕ — Law The symbol $$ \mathsf{\ᗕ} $$ represents the rule governing evolution. A law is a map $$ F:X\rightarrow X $$ or, more generally, $$ F:X\times T\rightarrow X. $$ The central philosophical distinction is: $$ \boxed{ \text{State} \neq \text{Law}. } $$ The state describes **what is**. The law describes **how it changes**. --- # IV. ߉ — Measurement The symbol $$ \mathsf{߉} $$ represents observation. A physical or mathematical system may possess a state $$ x\in X $$ while observation produces only $$ y=H(x). $$ Thus: $$ \boxed{ \text{Reality} \rightarrow \text{Measurement} \rightarrow \text{Representation}. } $$ The observer may therefore see only a projection of the underlying mathematical state. --- # V. ⟍ — Invariant The symbol $$ \mathsf{\⟍} $$ represents what survives evolution. Suppose a quantity $$ I:X\rightarrow\mathbb R $$ satisfies $$ I(x(t))=C $$ for all \(t\). Then \(I\) is an invariant of the dynamics. Examples include conserved quantities such as energy, momentum, or angular momentum in appropriate mathematical models. The philosophical principle is: $$ \boxed{ \text{Change reveals invariance.} } $$ We discover what something fundamentally is by observing what remains unchanged while everything else moves. --- # VI. ᗒ — Reconstruction The second appearance of $$ \mathsf{ᗒ} $$ represents reconstruction. Given observations $$ y(t), $$ we attempt to infer the hidden state: $$ x(t). $$ This creates an inverse problem: $$ \boxed{ \text{Observation} \rightarrow \text{Hidden Structure}. } $$ Mathematics becomes a mechanism for reconstructing invisible structure from observable consequences. --- # VII. ᔕ — Structure The final symbol represents the recovered mathematical structure. The goal is not merely to predict one observation. It is to discover the deeper object: $$ \mathcal S=(X,F,H,I,\ldots). $$ Thus the entire process becomes: $$ \boxed{ \text{State} \rightarrow \text{Dynamics} \rightarrow \text{Measurement} \rightarrow \text{Invariant} \rightarrow \text{Reconstruction} \rightarrow \text{Structure}. } $$ --- # VIII. Mathematics as a Theory of Change Caltech ᗒ⟋ᗕ߉⟍ᗒᔕ rejects the idea that mathematics is primarily about static objects. A number may be static. But a differential equation describes an entire family of possible evolutions: $$ \frac{dx}{dt}=F(x). $$ A geometry may describe a space. A dynamical geometry describes how structures within that space evolve. Thus: $$ \boxed{ \text{Mathematics is not only the study of things; it is the study of lawful change.} } $$ --- # IX. Differential Equations Differential equations occupy a privileged philosophical position. Given $$ \frac{dx}{dt}=F(x), $$ the equation does not specify one state. It specifies a **space of possible trajectories**. A solution is a curve $$ x:\mathbb R\rightarrow X $$ satisfying the equation. Therefore: $$ \boxed{ \text{A law generates a geometry of possible futures.} } $$ --- # X. Phase Space Instead of asking where a particle is, Caltech asks where the entire system lies in phase space. For position and momentum: $$ (q,p)\in\mathcal P. $$ The dynamics generates a trajectory: $$ (q(t),p(t)). $$ The trajectory is not the fundamental object. The fundamental object is: $$ \boxed{ (\mathcal P,\Phi_t) } $$ where $$ \Phi_t:\mathcal P\rightarrow\mathcal P $$ is the evolution operator. --- # XI. Symmetry and Conservation Caltech ᗒ
