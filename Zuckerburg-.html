# Zuckerberg ᕂvᗒ⟍⫰ᐃ⟍⫰𐒨- ### The Philosophy of Mathematical Networks **Zuckerberg ᕂvᗒ⟍⫰ᐃ⟍⫰𐒨-** begins with: > **A system's intelligence is not determined only by its individual elements, but by the structure of the connections between them.** Its central question is: $$ \boxed{ \text{What becomes possible when mathematical objects become connected?} } $$ Its fundamental object is the network: $$ \boxed{ G=(V,E) } $$ where: $$ V=\text{nodes}, $$ $$ E=\text{connections}. $$ --- # I. ᕂ — Network The first symbol represents the network itself. A collection of isolated objects is: $$ V=\{v_1,v_2,\ldots,v_n\}. $$ Once connections appear: $$ E\subseteq V\times V, $$ the collection becomes a structure. Thus: $$ \boxed{ \text{Objects} + \text{Connections} = \text{Network}. } $$ --- # II. v — Value Connections can create value. Let the value of an isolated node be: $$ V(v_i). $$ The network may instead possess: $$ V(G). $$ In some systems: $$ V(G)> \sum_i V(v_i). $$ The difference comes from relationships. Thus: $$ \boxed{ \text{Network value} = \text{individual value} + \text{relational value}. } $$ --- # III. ᗒ — Discovery A network makes previously distant objects discoverable. Suppose: $$ d(v_i,v_j) $$ is graph distance. A new edge can transform: $$ d(v_i,v_j)\gg1 $$ into: $$ d(v_i,v_j)=1. $$ Therefore networks reduce structural distance. $$ \boxed{ \text{Connection} = \text{compression of relational distance}. } $$ --- # IV. ⟍ — Filtering A network does not merely connect everything to everything.
